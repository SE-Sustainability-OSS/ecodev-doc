Two-stage NPV derivation

Explicit growth through year T, followed by stable perpetual growth.

The original reverse-engineering framework was developed by Aswath Damodaran . It is adapted here to make the DCF derivation explicit and to distinguish the nominal growth rate during the forecast period from the perpetual growth rate.

1. Standard valuation identity

The enterprise value is the present value of all future free cash flows:

\[V=\sum_{t=1}^{+\infty}\frac{FCF_t}{(1+\omega)^t}.\]

Splitting the valuation at terminal year \(T\):

\[V=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{FCF_t}{(1+\omega)^t}+\frac{FCF_T}{(1+\omega)^T}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{1+g_\infty}{1+\omega}\right)^k.\]
Explicit period Growth rate \(g\) through year \(T\)
Stable period Perpetual growth \(g_\infty\) after year \(T\)
Cash conversion \(FCF_t=Y_t(1-\alpha)\)

2. Substitute the growth assumptions

If \(Y\) is year-0 revenue, then \(FCF_t=Y(1-\alpha)(1+g)^t\) for \(1\le t\le T\). The first stable-period cash flow is \(FCF_{T+1}=FCF_T(1+g_\infty)\).

\[V=Y(1-\alpha)\frac{1+g}{1+\omega}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(\frac{1+g}{1+\omega}\right)^t+Y(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{1+g}{1+\omega}\right)^T\frac{1+g_\infty}{\omega-g_\infty}.\]

3. Closed-form valuation

Evaluating the geometric series and simplifying gives:

\[\boxed{V=Y(1-\alpha)\frac{1+g}{\omega-g}\left[1-\left(\frac{1+g}{1+\omega}\right)^{T-1}\frac{g-g_\infty}{\omega-g_\infty}\right]}\]

4. Solve for required revenue

The calculator rearranges the expression to isolate \(Y\):

\[\boxed{Y=\frac{V}{(1-\alpha)\dfrac{1+g}{\omega-g}\left[1-\left(\dfrac{1+g}{1+\omega}\right)^{T-1}\dfrac{g-g_\infty}{\omega-g_\infty}\right]}}\]

Conditions: \(\omega>g_\infty\) is required for the perpetual series to converge. The app also handles the limiting case \(g=\omega\) by computing the explicit-period sum directly rather than dividing by \(\omega-g\).
